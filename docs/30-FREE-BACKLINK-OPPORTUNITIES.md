## Primary-policy findings that changed the plan

- Product Hunt, G2 and DevHunt explicitly offer a free entry route. Hashnode explicitly offers a free hosted blog. Uneed has a conditional free waiting line and excludes some low-effort converters. HackerNoon mentions a first free brand credit; later promotion is not assumed free. These facts do not bypass account, agreement, editorial or metric gates.
- Hacker News prohibits generated text and automated posting. freeCodeCamp and DZone prohibit AI-written final articles. Those routes are rejected for this agent-written campaign; no account workaround or ghostwriting is proposed.
- DEV permits disclosed AI content but restricts business promotion and backlink-led posts. No generated FileCompresser promotion is prepared for DEV.
- SitePoint now requires Premium for a guest submission. It is excluded from the free route. Codrops charges for commercial Discover submissions; a free tool is not automatically non-commercial under its rules. No paid entry is recommended.
- A List Apart, Smashing, Hongkiat and InfoQ make editorial relevance central. Their contribution pages are not promises of a product link. DigitalOcean still displays an old pause notice. OpenReplay's invitation is dated. These remain holds rather than ready placements.
- CSS-Tricks, Webdesigner Depot, MakeUseOf and How-To Geek contribution pages were unavailable to the research tool. Their publisher traffic estimates do not prove a current free submission program. No access restriction was bypassed.

## Quality and traffic boundaries

Official contribution, product and policy pages were inspected through web research. Current exact external-link attributes were not inspected on thirty live placement examples, and no complete malware/PBN audit, owner identity certification or full article-cadence crawl was performed. None of these sites is certified free of spam by this register. No unrelated directory, link exchange, paid insert, bulk account or comment campaign was used to fill the count.

The provider pages show search visibility, but traffic can be uneven and estimates can disagree. For example, Semrush showed Product Hunt organic search at 432,100 with +2.28% month-on-month, while its separately measured total visits fell 30.24%; those are different series. How-To Geek's displayed organic estimate fell 57.04%, so it should not be waved through merely because its absolute estimate exceeds 1,000. No suspicious cause is inferred from a one-month change. Country-level organic traffic, relevant page traffic and longer trends need provider access before prioritizing outreach spend or time.
